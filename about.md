# Jack of all trades, Master in Robotics

I am, first and foremost, an engineer at heart. I consider technology an expression of the human mind and deeply enjoy learning new things that I can apply to solving problems. Or sometimes, just for fun.

![my_picture](/assets/images/remu3.jpeg)

I completed my bachelor's in the Universidad de los Andes in Bogotá, Colombia. This university is amongst the best in the continent, but it was by no means cheap. Therefore, I worked as a maths and science tutor while I studied. I am also curious to a fault and wanted to take part in several extracurricular initiatives, which meant that I learned to manage my time very efficiently.

This program had a very applied and pragmatic approach. We grew comfortable with experimental verification and calibration and I learned to use knowledge as a basis to design, troubleshoot, and make decisions, not only to pass exams. I learned important skills that cannot be taught in a classroom: translating functional requirements and project restrictions to design decisions in software or hardware, especially when stringent time or budget constraints could dictate the feasibility of the entire ordeal. I also became proficient in different creative techniques and design principles: from lab-controlled electromechanical component and material characterization, to computer-assisted design, to iterative and hands-on rapid prototyping. I learned to adjust according to the situation at hand rather than following a script, pivoting between disciplines and approaches depending on the current need.

I am very aware that technology does not exist in a vacuum, but is created for and by humans. Hence, I also underwent additional education in human-centered design and project development.  In fact, the topic of expanding the frontier of human-robot cooperation became a particular interest of mine, and I decided to further my education in that direction. While undergoing an interdisciplinary robotics program at TUM, I also complemented my lessons with work experience in my field to ground the theory I was learning and appreciate the nuances that come with embodied systems, troubleshooting solutions for problems that cannot be foreseen in a simulation only.
