Design of a low-cost compliant foot prosthetic designed for traversing irregular terrain. Semester project for the class Neuroprosthetics (2022) TUM.

This semester-long project involved the design and first iteration of a foot prosthesis prototype. The motivation behind this design stems from the consecuences of the armed conflict in Colombia, after which a considerable amount of people, mostly Inhabitants from remote rural communities, lost their lives or limbs to anti-personnel mines. The design of this soft foot adresses the unique necessities of people who earn ther livelyhood by cultivating crops on the hillsides with steep, irregular terrain.

The most important aspects of the design relies on its mechanical simplicity. The flexible foot was modeled to provide similar dorsiflexion and plantar flexion to the naturally occurring values during the normal human gait cycle. The heel also provides controlled inversion-eversion mobility and the sole is also designed to deform locally around common irregularities (i.e. pebbles).
